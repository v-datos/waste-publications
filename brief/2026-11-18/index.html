Key takeaway

Build the measurement plan before changing the service. Canmore's draft capital plan schedules scale automation for 2027; this is a chance to specify how tickets and tonnage will be reconciled at cutover. Sylvan Lake's draft puts new organics carts ahead of a later reduction in garbage collection frequency. Set a baseline for each change so that different equipment, routes or households served do not get mistaken for higher diversion. Canmore Finance Committee package · Sylvan Lake budget package

Alberta

Canmore puts scale automation in the draft capital plan

What changed. The revised November 12 Finance Committee package schedules scale automation for 2027 and indicates reserve funding in the capital planning summary. It lists the solid waste operating and capital model as a verbal item for information. The schedule is proposed; the agenda does not establish approval, commissioning or a change in household rates. Town of Canmore, revised November 12 package, item D7 and p. 41

Why it matters and what to watch. The November 19 agenda lists preliminary deliberations on capital budget amendments. If the scale project proceeds, ask staff to name the records that will carry across the change, including ticket ID, facility, hauler, stream, gross, tare, net weight and any manual override. Compare old and new records against invoices for the same loads and record the cutover date in the monthly series. This gives Canmore and any Bow Valley partners using the data a way to distinguish a recording change from a shift in tonnage. Town of Canmore, November 19 agenda, item D1

Sylvan Lake sequences organics carts before changing garbage frequency

What changed. The November 12 budget presentation proposes buying green carts in 2027 and starting contractor collection in May 2028. The proposed organics schedule is weekly for about seven months and then every two weeks. Every-two-week garbage service would follow in late 2028 or early 2029, after residents have had time to adjust. The presentation marks both service changes as requiring council approval. The town already offered seasonal curbside yard and kitchen waste collection in 2026. Town of Sylvan Lake, budget package, pp. 100–103 · Town of Sylvan Lake, current collection guidance

Why it matters and what to watch. The same draft holds the blue cart rate at $4.50 per month, while staff expect producer funding for at least one of the town's two recycling depots to end on December 31, 2027. The presentation gives $111,000 a year for both depots together; the amount at risk for either site is unspecified. Watch the adopted capital and rate schedules, and ask which site and services would lose funding before assuming a closure or budgeting a replacement amount. For the organics change, compare the same seasons before and after rollout; record eligible households, collection frequency, garbage and accepted organics tonnes, and missed pickups. Town of Sylvan Lake, budget package, pp. 100–102

Jasper asks staff to reweight the biosolids options

What changed. Minutes attached to the November 17 council package record that the Committee of the Whole directed staff on November 10 to develop implementation plans for the three highest-rated biosolids options and report back. The committee separately directed staff to assign 40% of the scoring weight to cost and divide the remaining 60% equally among the other criteria. The earlier staff report identifies the leading option in each of three pathways under its original assessment: landfill burial, modular low-temperature heat-pump drying, and land application at reclamation or mine sites. The committee direction is a planning step; the minutes record no technology selection. Municipality of Jasper, November 17 package, motions 486/26 and 488/26 · November 10 staff report, item 7.1

Why it matters and what to watch. Ask for the revised scoring and the effect of different hauling, moisture and receiving-site assumptions before comparing lifetime costs. Use dry tonnes to compare solids managed and wet tonnes to explain hauling requirements. The options concern wastewater biosolids; keep their budget and destination records separate from household organics.

Jasper reports separate milestones for debris removal and site clearance

What changed. The recovery update in Jasper's November 17 package includes a Parks Canada snapshot dated November 9. For the 375 properties tracked within the municipality, it shows zero properties with debris remaining, three with confirmatory soil sampling reports under Parks Canada review, and 372 with certificates of completion. The adjacent demolition-permit tally covers properties in and out of town and uses a different denominator. Jasper Recovery Coordination Centre, November 17 update, pp. 3–4

Why it matters and what to watch. Use separate dates for haul-out, sample review and certificate issuance in incident reporting. Follow the remaining three reviews. These are property status counts; they cannot show how many tonnes were processed or recovered.

Broader Canada

Hornepayne starts a new household recycling schedule

What changed. The Ontario township announced that its new residential recycling bin service would start November 17, every other Tuesday. Its launch notice also lists a recycling depot at the landfill site with separate operating hours. The notices establish the service and schedule; they provide no pickup completion or recovery data. Township of Hornepayne, dated program notice · Township launch notice

Why it matters and what to watch. Check the first route's eligible addresses, cart delivery exceptions and missed pickups. Track depot visits and curbside set-outs separately. A shift in where residents bring the same material can move counts between channels even if total recycling is unchanged. Check processor intake and rejects before interpreting any increase in collected weight.

Port Clements lists proposed regional waste fees

What changed. Port Clements' November 16 agenda lists, for information, proposed changes from British Columbia's North Coast Regional District. Examples include garbage bag tags from $2 to $4 each and commercial recycling pickup, where available, from $20 to $25 per bag. The regional district says the board will revisit the proposal on November 20; the changes would take effect January 1, 2027 if adopted. Village of Port Clements, November 16 agenda, item C-1 · North Coast Regional District, proposed rate schedule

Why it matters and what to watch. Compare household and commercial charges under their own service definitions. If the board adopts the changes, follow bag-tag sales and transfer-station loads alongside collected tonnes to see whether volumes shift between outlets. A fee per bag cannot be compared directly with a fee per tonne without a representative weight per bag.

Practical actions for Banff and Canmore

Decision Record to request
Accept scale data after a system change Reconcile ticket and invoice totals by stream and facility. Track missing tickets, duplicate loads and manual corrections across the cutover.
Evaluate organics routes Compare like seasons and households eligible for the same service. Report commercial and tourism sites separately using serviced accounts; record occupancy or another activity measure if used.
Budget for a depot funding change Record each site's funding end date, accepted streams, annual cost and potential capacity at alternative sites before modelling a change in access.
Report performance through a service or facility change Keep supplied, collected, accepted, recycled or composted, stored and disposed quantities distinct. Reconcile rejects and inventory movement before reporting a recovery rate.
