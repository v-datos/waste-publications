Key takeaway

Treat a service announcement, a budget proposal and a measured result as separate milestones. This week's municipal records offer practical decisions about fees, depot access, winter collection routes and enforcement. For Banff and Canmore, the immediate priority is to document who receives each service, what unit is charged and what happens to material after collection before attributing a change to better diversion.

Alberta

Canmore brings forward its proposed master fee schedule

What changed. The November 24 Finance Committee package seeks a recommendation to Council to approve the 2027-2028 Master Fee Schedule. Staff used anticipated inflation of 2.3% for 2027 and 2.0% for 2028 as guidance. These are planning assumptions, not a uniform increase established for every service. The schedule distinguishes waste transfer charges per tonne, commercial food-waste collection per cart tip, commercial collection per hour and a contaminated-recycling-load penalty per tonne. The package establishes a proposal, not Council adoption. Town of Canmore, November 24 package, item D2, pp. 9-10 and 15

Why it matters; watch next. Before updating a Bow Valley cost model, obtain the adopted schedule and effective dates. Model haulage, transfer and contamination charges separately, retaining their original billing units. Applying one inflation factor to all charges could misstate the cost of a particular service mix. This is a different decision from last week's proposed scale automation project.

Camrose plans permanent hazardous-waste access and changes billing authority

What changed. Camrose's November 24 highlights of its November 23 meetings say Council directed staff to develop a household hazardous-waste depot plan at the Recycle Depot, with a detailed proposal to return later. This would replace twice-yearly roundups; it is not an opening announcement. Staff also reported accessibility and bulky-item issues with new recycling-depot bins. City of Camrose, council highlights

The same summary records third reading of recycling and waste-collection amendments enabling a transition to owner-only utility accounts by December 31, 2029. By contrast, the 2027 fees bylaw received first reading, with solid-waste and recycling fees proposed to stay unchanged. The highlights are an official summary, not approved minutes.

Why it matters; watch next. Request the depot's accepted-material list, operating plan and cost before planning year-round access. Track bin-access complaints separately from recovered material. For billing changes, reconcile owners and serviced premises; an account change alone does not measure waste reduction.

Broader Canada

Golden landfill consultation separates authorized capacity from actual use

What changed. A November 23 environmental protection notice proposes an amended operational certificate for the existing Golden Landfill in British Columbia. It describes a smaller authorized footprint, liners and leachate collection for future phases within that footprint, discharge to the municipal sewage collection system, and a higher annual waste authorization to accommodate growth, variability and unforeseen events. These are proposed certificate changes, not evidence of completed construction or increased actual disposal. Environmental Protection Notice, November 23

Why it matters. For a mountain municipality assessing disposal resilience, a permitted annual tonnage, remaining physical capacity and actual annual receipts answer different questions. An allowance for disaster debris does not establish available surge space, waste acceptance or a disposal agreement for another community. Treat Golden as a regional planning example, not as an identified outlet for Bow Valley waste.

Watch next. The CSRD lists January 22, 2027 as the comment deadline. The notice says the Director intends to issue the amended certificate no sooner than 60 days after its November 23 date; it does not promise issuance on the deadline. Comments should reference authorization 17006 and follow the notice's submission instructions. Review the final certificate and implementation requirements before changing a capacity or cost forecast. CSRD, public notice and supporting documents

West Nipissing announces recycling routes distinct from garbage routes

What changed. West Nipissing's November 24 notice says blue-box collection will begin in December for parts of Field, Kipling and River Valley already receiving curbside garbage service. Circular Materials and GFL will provide collection every two weeks, with different routes from household garbage. Some households will use designated common collection locations because of road conditions, snowbanks or restricted manoeuvring space. The municipality says carts or boxes have been delivered and affected households will receive letters identifying their collection location. Municipality of West Nipissing, November 24 notice

Why it matters; watch next. Verify start dates and locations from household letters rather than copying a garbage-route map. Record households assigned to common points separately from those with collection at their property. Watch first-cycle missed pickups, access problems and resident enquiries. The announcement gives no completed-collection or recycling-output results; delivered boxes alone cannot establish participation.

Greenstone reaches its announced curbside recycling launch week

What changed. Greenstone's October 2 notice scheduled residential curbside recycling to begin November 23, inside this reporting window. It identifies Circular Materials as the program administrator and directs collection and bin questions there. The municipality explicitly says this is not its own service. The older notice establishes the planned launch date; it does not confirm that every first-week pickup occurred. Municipality of Greenstone, launch notice

Why it matters; watch next. Assign customer enquiries to the correct service provider and request first-cycle completion and exception records. For analysis, mark the change in service availability before comparing depot and curbside volumes. Moving material from depot delivery to curbside can change channel totals without demonstrating a rise in total recycling. This residential launch does not establish collection entitlement for commercial or tourism premises.

Owen Sound puts downtown set-out rules into enforcement

What changed. Owen Sound's November 18 notice identifies that Wednesday as the first collection day under full enforcement of updated downtown waste rules. Property owners must supply or display the current waste calendar; set-outs must be beside their originating property; uncollected waste must be removed by 8 p.m. The city says it can invoice an adjacent owner when staff remove improperly placed waste, with an appeal available where evidence shows placement without the consent of the owner, occupant or guest. These are Owen Sound requirements, not Bow Valley rules. City of Owen Sound, November 18 notice

Why it matters; watch next. The lesson for visitor-oriented commercial streets is to retain location, time, notification and appeal evidence when assigning cleanup costs. Track removals, invoices and successful appeals separately. The notice describes enforcement, not a measured reduction in illegal dumping. It also gives November 17 as the enforcement start in its quick facts; November 18 is the explicit first collection-day milestone used here.

Practical analytics and operational actions

The following are analytical recommendations drawn from these cases, not new requirements for Banff or Canmore.

Decision Immediate record or check Interpretation to preserve
Update a waste-service budget Join each adopted charge to its service code, billing unit and effective date; apply it to the corresponding activity. A per-tonne fee, per-tip fee and hourly charge cannot be substituted for one another.
Evaluate depot access Record opening days, accepted materials, user eligibility, accessibility complaints and material received. An announced facility or delivered container is not evidence of material recovered.
Compare collection through a route change Keep the eligible-address list, collection method, schedule and completion exceptions for each stream. Changing households served or moving material between depot and curbside creates a break in the series.
Assess regional disposal resilience Request actual receipts, remaining capacity, authorization conditions and contingency arrangements separately. A higher annual authorization does not itself establish spare capacity available to another municipality.
Report diversion and enforcement Keep collected, accepted, recycled or composted, stored and disposed quantities distinct; retain rejects and inventory movements. Record cleanup and appeal outcomes separately. Collected tonnes and enforcement activity do not by themselves show recycling output or reduced dumping.

For the Bow Valley, separate residential service accounts from commercial and tourism premises. If visitor activity is used to normalize a series, disclose the activity measure and keep it consistent across periods. No new local performance estimate is calculated in this edition.
