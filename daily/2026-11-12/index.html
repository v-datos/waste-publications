The takeaway Canada backed a COP31 declaration that puts keeping organics out of landfills alongside capturing methane from waste already buried.

Canada backs a COP31 declaration on methane

Canada endorsed the November 11 Global Methane Pledge ministerial declaration in Antalya. It reaffirmed a voluntary, collective goal to cut global human-caused methane emissions at least 30% below 2020 levels by 2030. For waste, the declaration calls for keeping organics out of landfill, expanding resource recovery and capturing methane where emissions cannot yet be avoided. Global Methane Pledge ministerial declaration.

Landfill methane can come from organic material deposited over decades. Gas collection can address those emissions even as a diversion program changes what the landfill receives. For a regional waste plan, track organic tonnes still sent to landfill alongside methane captured and treated at the receiving site. Environment and Climate Change Canada.

IEA puts organics treatment in a wider emissions picture

In its November 11 report for COP31, the International Energy Agency estimated that a global package of waste prevention and material efficiency, better collection and recycling, and sustainable energy recovery could cut annual greenhouse gas emissions by more than 1.5 gigatonnes of CO₂ equivalent by 2035. That estimate spans multiple sectors and depends on broad adoption. For individual projects, the report calls for comparing options under local conditions and flags methane leaks as a factor in anaerobic digestion performance. IEA report, Figure 5 and pp. 37, 42.

Worth acting on For a Bow Valley screening comparison, use the same tonnage and mix of organics across the current disposal route and any feasible composting or digestion routes. Use the actual haul distance for each route and the receiving landfill’s gas recovery practices. Canada’s organics greenhouse gas calculator supports those inputs and estimates emissions for each scenario. Environment and Climate Change Canada calculator.
