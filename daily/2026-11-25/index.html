The takeaway Moose Jaw's removal of glass from residential blue bins shows why a collection change needs an explicit alternative destination; West Nipissing's expansion also makes the location of service part of the message.

At a glance

  • Moose Jaw will stop accepting glass containers in residential blue bins on December 1; a SARCAN drop-off route remains available.
  • Parts of Field, Kipling and River Valley will gain blue box collection in December, with designated common collection locations where needed.
  • A waste-image study makes a useful distinction: a confident disagreement can reveal a questionable label, but it does not establish the dataset's overall error rate.

Moose Jaw redirects glass to a depot from December 1

In a November 24 notice, Moose Jaw, Saskatchewan, announced that glass containers will no longer be accepted in residential blue bins from December 1, 2026. The city says glass frequently breaks during collection and attributes the change to sorting-staff safety and contamination prevention. Those are the city's stated reasons; the notice provides no measured reduction in injuries or contamination. City announcement.

Clear and coloured glass bottles and jars, whether deposit or non-deposit, can still go to the SARCAN Depot at 52 Highland Road. The city lists beverage bottles, condiment bottles, cooking-oil containers and food jars, and asks residents to rinse them and remove lids.

For Banff/Canmore communications, the transferable lesson is to publish the replacement route beside the exclusion. This is a Moose Jaw service change, not a change to Bow Valley acceptance rules. Monitoring should distinguish glass diverted to the depot from glass shifted into garbage; the blue-bin exclusion alone does not establish a better recovery outcome.

West Nipissing adds recycling, with winter access designed into the route

West Nipissing, Ontario, announced November 24 that blue box collection will begin in December 2026 for households in parts of Field, Kipling and River Valley that currently receive curbside garbage service. Circular Materials and GFL Environmental will implement a two-week collection schedule. Recycling routes will differ from garbage routes. Municipal notice.

Common collection locations have been established where road conditions, snowbanks or limited space hinder vehicle manoeuvring. Affected households will receive letters identifying their designated location and guidelines; the municipality says carts or boxes have already been delivered. The notice does not specify a single first-pickup date for all affected households.

For a mountain municipality, this is a useful service-design distinction: counting households covered by recycling is different from counting households with pickup immediately outside their property. Track designated collection location and winter accessibility alongside participation, and make household instructions specific to the recycling route.

Research spotlight: audit the labels before choosing a waste classifier

Keumala and colleagues' November 11 paper compares ResNet18, MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0 on three image classes: recyclable, electronic and organic. MobileNetV2 reports 92.68% accuracy on 2,651 validation images. The same validation split selected training checkpoints, and the authors caution that a single run and split do not establish a decisive model ranking. These are image-benchmark results, not demonstrated sorting performance in a municipal facility. Full paper, methods, Table 2 and discussion.

One rater then examined MobileNetV2's 40 most confident wrong predictions: 12 were judged probable label errors, 14 ambiguous and 14 model errors. The 30% probable-label-error share applies only to that deliberately selected group, not to the full dataset. The finding supports investigating labels; it does not justify automatically replacing them with model predictions.

Worth acting on For a local camera-classification pilot, pair a review of confident disagreements with a separate representative sample and labels based on the actual local acceptance rules. Keep unresolved cases visible, and evaluate on a local holdout that was not used to select the model. Benchmark accuracy alone cannot establish operational benefit.
