The takeaway Québec's opposition is seeking changes to the next deposit-return phase; municipalities should keep political proposals separate from the published implementation timetable when planning material flows.

At a glance

  • Québec Liberals seek a two-year delay for wine and spirits bottles and cancellation of the multilayer-carton expansion; this is an opposition request.
  • GFL seeks a replacement storage building near North Bay with the same permitted storage and daily intake limits; Ontario's consultation closes January 9, 2027.
  • A composition-modelling study reports uneven predictive performance across materials, despite using log-ratios and a larger dataset.

Québec opposition asks to change the deposit-return expansion

On November 25, Québec Liberal environment spokesperson Virginie Dufour called for postponing deposits on wine and spirits bottles by two years and permanently cancelling the expansion to multilayer cartons, including milk and juice cartons. She also requested an independent audit of Consignaction covering return-point deployment, service, finances and governance. These are political demands, not an adopted amendment. Party statement.

The expansion date at issue is March 1, 2027. Consignaction's published timetable places newly covered glass and multilayer beverage containers in that phase. Operator timetable, published December 19, 2024.

For municipal material-flow scenarios, record the opposition's requested changes as a separate scenario. The statement does not establish that deposits or blue-bin instructions have changed. Its calls for cost comparisons and measurable performance are questions for evaluation, rather than evidence that either collection system is cheaper or recovers more material.

Ontario opens consultation on GFL's replacement storage building

Ontario posted proposal ERO 026-1220 on November 25 for GFL's transfer/processing site at Commanda, near North Bay. The application would replace the former 2,700 m² waste storage building with an approximately 1,860 m² structure. The notice lists existing approval limits of 400 tonnes stored at any one time and 80 tonnes received per day, with no proposed change to either limit or the material types. Unloading would remain indoors. Comments close January 9, 2027, at 11:59 p.m., as stated on the registry. Proposal and consultation.

For facility comparisons, keep building area, permitted inventory and daily throughput in separate fields. A smaller building footprint does not, by itself, establish lower permitted throughput, faster turnover or better processing performance. This is a proposed building replacement; the notice is not approval of the work.

Research spotlight: a better composition model still needs material-level scrutiny

Abu Orabi, Escobedo and Özaltın's study, indexed in October 2026 and assigned to the December issue of Waste Management Bulletin, combines a log-ratio transformation, an Extra Trees regressor and composition-consistent imputation. The authors' abstract describes 115 observations across 33 U.S. states and 43 material categories. It reports a variance-weighted R² of 0.29 in transformed space over an evaluation using 20 train-test splits. That is not 29% classification accuracy. Paper and authors' abstract.

The abstract reports stronger predictions for newspaper, glossy paper and office paper than for film plastic and electronics. Simply aggregating subcategories into broader groups did not improve predictions in their analysis. The authors instead propose retaining reliably predicted materials individually and grouping the remainder. These are abstract-reported findings from U.S. composition data, not a locally validated Banff/Canmore forecast.

Worth acting on For a local composition model, inspect errors by material and document the transformation and evaluation split before choosing the reporting categories. Test whether grouping actually helps locally; a single overall score can conceal weak predictions for a material that matters to a collection or procurement decision.
