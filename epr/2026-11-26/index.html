Executive brief

  • NEW · British Columbia infrastructure. Recycle BC announced construction underway on a Delta sorting facility, with completion expected in 2028. Its approximately 120,000 tonnes/year is design capacity for residential packaging and paper, not operating throughput. Operator announcement
  • NEW · Western municipal access. Camrose is developing a household hazardous-waste depot proposal. Moose Jaw will stop accepting glass in residential blue bins on December 1 and directs residents to SARCAN. The first is planning; the second is an announced collection change. Camrose · Moose Jaw
  • NEW · BC depot finance dispute. The depot association challenges proposed handling payments; Encorp disputes the financial assessment. This is a service-continuity risk to investigate, without evidence here of particular imminent closures. Association release · CKPG interviews
  • ONGOING · Regulatory decisions. Ontario's Blue Box consultation closes on release day, November 26. Alberta oversight fees and BC battery-plan additions remain under consultation. Draft rules and charges should retain that status in municipal advice. Ontario · ARMA · Call2Recycle

Regulatory developments by jurisdiction

Alberta · ONGOING oversight-fee consultation

ARMA continues to list draft 2027 individual-producer oversight charges of CAD 180 plus CAD 0.0307/kg for PPP, and CAD 325 plus CAD 0.0733/kg for HSP, subject to the applicable supply threshold for variable fees. PPP means single-use products, packaging and paper products; HSP means hazardous and special products. Comments close December 11 at 11:59 p.m. ABT, as posted. These are oversight charges, distinct from a PRO's collection and processing charges. Municipal budget comparisons should not equate the draft rates with local contract savings. ARMA, draft fee tables and consultation

Ontario · ONGOING Blue Box proposal; comment period ends today

Environmental Registry notice 026-1058 remains a proposal, with comments due November 26 at 11:59 p.m., as posted. Options concern PRO fee transparency and contracts, flexible-plastic obligations, overflow collection and counting small industrial, commercial and institutional (IC&I) material toward producer targets. The ministry envisages changes in 2027, conditional on legislative passage and regulatory development. Counting business material would not itself guarantee collection for every business. Municipal submissions should specify the affected service, material and cost allocation. Ministry proposal

The inspected Assembly status table for Bill 140 still lists November 17 committee consideration as its latest entry. That record does not establish Royal Assent or a new effective collection rule. This bulletin records no verified legislative-stage advance during the coverage window. Continue to use current local collection instructions while monitoring the bill and subsequent regulations separately. Assembly bill record

British Columbia · ONGOING battery-plan amendments

Call2Recycle's consultation covers proposed additions of vapes/e-cigarettes, 12-volt batteries and portable power to its BC battery plan. Written feedback remains due December 8, 5 p.m. PCT, as posted. The page describes consultation, without establishing approval of the additions. Municipal receiving sites can seek clear acceptance, equipment, staffing and payment terms before revising their own instructions. Operator consultation

Program and municipal operations

Alberta · NEW Camrose develops a permanent-depot proposal

The city's November 24 summary of November 23 meetings says Council directed administration to develop a household hazardous-waste depot plan at the Recycle Depot, with a detailed proposal to return for consideration. It would replace twice-yearly roundups; no opening date or approved operating budget is given. The summary also reports accessibility and bulky-item concerns with new depot bins, while staff say most issues with the broader-material, biweekly curbside program have been addressed. These are council highlights, explicitly not official minutes, and staff assessments rather than quantified service results. For Alberta peers, the next useful evidence is the detailed depot proposal and its service and funding arrangements. City of Camrose

Saskatchewan · NEW Moose Jaw redirects glass to a depot

Moose Jaw's November 24 notice removes glass containers from residential blue-bin collection from December 1, 2026. It directs residents to the SARCAN Depot, 52 Highland Road, for clear and coloured deposit and non-deposit bottles and jars, rinsed with lids removed. The city cites broken glass, sorting-staff safety and contamination as reasons; it provides no measured injury or contamination reduction. Update material guides and depot referrals together so that a curbside exclusion is communicated with its alternative route. This local notice does not establish a province-wide glass exclusion. City instructions

ONGOING · Provincial transition calendar. SK Recycles still lists December 1 as the final planned community-led Phase 2 launch date. Its program-led timetable anticipates provider selection in November 2026 and service commencement on February 1, 2028. The inspected timetable names no awarded provider. Confirm the local service model and contract before communicating a handover; these are different transition paths. SK Recycles timetable

Ontario · NEW West Nipissing route instructions; NEW Greenstone reminder

West Nipissing: the November 24 notice announces December blue-box service for households in parts of Field, Kipling and River Valley already receiving curbside garbage collection. Circular Materials and GFL will use a two-week schedule, with routes differing from garbage. Some households will use common collection locations because of road conditions, snowbanks or manoeuvring limits; affected households will receive individual location letters. The municipality says carts/boxes have been delivered. Referral staff should use those letters rather than assume that garbage set-out points also apply to recycling. Municipal notice

Greenstone: a November 20 reminder announces collection beginning the week of November 23, administered by Circular Materials and collected by Emterra. It instructs residents to keep paper, cardboard, flexible plastic and laminated paper separate from other eligible recyclables, and set out by 7 a.m. on their regular garbage day every two weeks. A separate vehicle collects recycling, potentially at a different time. This is a new operational reminder for a previously announced launch, not confirmation that every first-round pickup occurred. Municipal reminder

Ontario · UPDATED North Bay announces reopening

Following last week's closure, North Bay's November 19 advisory schedules its Waste Diversion Facility reopening for November 20, with household hazardous waste, electronics, scrap metal, leaf and yard waste, and residential recycling drop-off returning. It gives regular hours of 8 a.m.-6 p.m. at 112 Patton Street. The advisory updates the prior closure information; it is an announced restoration of service, without a post-reopening operating report or a finding on the fire's cause. City advisory

Quebec · NEW Côte-Saint-Luc return location and user-survey evidence

Consignaction's November 19 release announces a Consignaction+ site at 5800 boulevard Cavendish, unit H2, in Côte-Saint-Luc's Quartier Cavendish centre. It serves deposit-bearing beverage containers and identifies residents and IC&I users among its clientele. Municipal directories can identify this specific return route without implying that the site accepts all business packaging. The release supplies no measured throughput. Operator opening announcement

In its November 20 release, Consignaction reports that 93% of surveyed users intend to return, while three-quarters are satisfied or very satisfied overall. Its 89% figure combines satisfaction and neutral responses. The Ad hoc Recherche Web-panel survey covered 2,032 adults, September 17-29, who lived within 5 km of a site, knew the network and had returned deposit containers in the previous six months. This is an operator-commissioned study of eligible users, not a province-wide access or recovery measure. For municipal evaluation, pair user feedback with evidence about non-users, distance and actual material returns. Operator release and survey criteria

Infrastructure, end markets and financial signals

British Columbia · NEW Delta materials recovery facility under construction

Recycle BC's November 19 announcement describes a 145,000-square-foot facility at 9336 River Road, to be designed, built and operated by Miller Waste Systems, with completion expected in 2028. It follows Recycle BC's June 2026 move to direct post-collection management. Optical sorting is planned. The release establishes a construction milestone and projected capacity, without operating recovery yields, residue rates or end-market sales. For municipal procurement, request commissioning milestones and verified output destinations before assigning future capacity to a service plan. Recycle BC announcement

British Columbia · NEW depot-payment dispute needs local continuity checks

BCBRDA's November 19 release launched Reject-It.ca. The association says Encorp's November 12 offer freezes handling fees for 2027-2031 if accepted by December 15, with cuts otherwise; it warns of reduced service and closures. These are an interested party's account and projections. Association release via CNW

CKPG's November 23 reporting includes Encorp's response that its proposed rates allow a reasonable return; the parties dispute the financial assessment and explanations for earlier depot closures. The inspected evidence does not identify which depots will close or when. Municipalities that refer multiple material streams to independent depots should check local acceptance and continuity arrangements. A beverage-handling fee dispute alone does not establish termination of every other recycling service at those sites. CKPG, interviews with both parties

What to watch next

  • Alberta municipal planning: the detailed Camrose depot proposal, including operating responsibilities and funding, and ARMA's eventual final fee schedule. Define household, business and existing stewardship-program eligibility separately in service agreements.
  • Western service continuity: local confirmation of Saskatchewan transition arrangements, depot acceptance and hours, and the terms of BC's proposed new battery streams. The depot-payment dispute warrants direct checks with receiving sites.
  • Evidence of delivery: first-round collection results in newly served Ontario communities, actual throughput at return sites and commissioning evidence for new processing infrastructure. Keep supplied, collected, recycled, stored and disposed quantities separate; require a period and denominator for performance comparisons.
